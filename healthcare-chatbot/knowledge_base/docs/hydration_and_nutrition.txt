TITLE: Hydration and Balanced Nutrition Basics
SOURCE: NIH / MedlinePlus (adapted educational summary)
---
Staying well hydrated supports nearly every body function, including
digestion, temperature regulation, joint lubrication, and nutrient
transport. A commonly cited general guideline is roughly 8 cups (about 2
liters) of fluids per day for adults, though individual needs vary based on
activity level, climate, body size, and health conditions such as kidney or
heart disease — people with such conditions should follow their doctor's
specific fluid guidance rather than a general rule of thumb.

Signs of good hydration include pale-yellow urine, moist lips, and steady
energy levels. Signs of dehydration include dark urine, dizziness,
headache, dry mouth, and fatigue; severe dehydration (confusion, rapid
heartbeat, very little urination) needs urgent medical attention.

A balanced diet generally includes a variety of:
- Vegetables and fruits (a wide range of colors for different nutrients)
- Whole grains (brown rice, whole wheat, oats, millet)
- Protein sources (legumes, lentils, eggs, fish, lean meat, dairy, tofu)
- Healthy fats (nuts, seeds, olive oil)
- Limited added sugar, refined carbohydrates, and ultra-processed foods
- Moderate salt intake to support healthy blood pressure

Practical everyday tips:
- Build meals around vegetables and whole grains, with a palm-sized
  portion of protein.
- Choose water over sugary drinks most of the time.
- Eat mindfully and avoid skipping meals, which can lead to overeating
  later.
- Read nutrition labels to be aware of added sugar and sodium.

Specific dietary plans for medical conditions (diabetes, kidney disease,
food allergies, pregnancy, etc.) should be personalized with a doctor or
registered dietitian, since individual nutrient needs and restrictions vary
considerably.
