TITLE: Fever and the Common Cold
SOURCE: CDC (adapted educational summary)
---
Fever is a temporary rise in body temperature, usually a sign that the body's
immune system is fighting an infection such as a virus or bacteria. In
adults, a temperature at or above 38°C (100.4°F) is generally considered a
fever. Mild fevers are common with colds and flu and typically resolve on
their own within a few days.

Common cold symptoms usually build gradually and include a runny or stuffy
nose, sore throat, mild cough, sneezing, and mild fatigue. Fever is less
common in adult colds but can occur, especially in children. The flu
(influenza) tends to come on more suddenly and cause higher fever, body
aches, chills, and more pronounced fatigue than a common cold.

General self-care for mild fever and cold symptoms:
- Rest and get adequate sleep to support the immune response.
- Drink plenty of fluids (water, clear broths, oral rehydration solutions)
  to prevent dehydration, especially if sweating from fever.
- Use a cool compress or lukewarm sponge bath for comfort if the fever
  feels uncomfortable.
- Keep the room at a comfortable temperature and dress lightly.
- Gargling warm salt water can soothe a sore throat.
- Steam inhalation or a humidifier may ease nasal congestion.

When to seek medical care:
- Fever above 39.4°C (103°F) in adults, or any fever in an infant under
  3 months old.
- Fever lasting more than 3 days without improvement.
- Fever accompanied by a stiff neck, severe headache, confusion, rash,
  difficulty breathing, persistent vomiting, or severe pain.
- Underlying chronic conditions (heart disease, diabetes, weakened immune
  system) with any new fever.

This information is educational and general. Exact treatment, and whether
any medication is appropriate, should always be discussed with a doctor or
pharmacist, particularly for children, pregnant individuals, and people with
chronic health conditions.
