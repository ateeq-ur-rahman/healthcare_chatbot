TITLE: Preventive Healthcare Essentials
SOURCE: WHO / CDC (adapted educational summary)
---
Preventive healthcare focuses on avoiding illness before it starts, or
catching problems early when they're most treatable. Core pillars widely
recommended by public health organizations include:

Routine screenings and check-ups:
- Regular blood pressure checks, since hypertension often has no symptoms.
- Periodic blood sugar and cholesterol testing, particularly with family
  history of diabetes or heart disease.
- Age- and risk-appropriate cancer screenings (e.g., cervical, breast,
  colorectal) as advised by a doctor.
- Regular dental and eye check-ups.

Vaccination:
- Staying up to date with recommended vaccines (seasonal flu, tetanus
  boosters, and others per local health guidelines) is one of the most
  effective preventive measures against many infectious diseases.
- Vaccine schedules vary by age, health status, and country — a doctor or
  local health authority can confirm what's currently recommended.

Lifestyle-based prevention:
- Regular physical activity (commonly cited target: about 150 minutes of
  moderate activity per week for adults).
- Not smoking, and limiting alcohol consumption.
- Maintaining a balanced diet and healthy body weight.
- Good hand hygiene and food safety practices to reduce infection risk.
- Wearing seatbelts/helmets and following workplace safety practices to
  prevent injury.

Mental health as prevention:
- Regular check-ins on mental wellbeing are as important as physical
  screenings; early support for stress, anxiety, or low mood can prevent
  more serious issues later.

None of the above replaces a personalized preventive care plan. Screening
schedules and vaccination needs depend on age, sex, family history, and
existing conditions, so it's best to discuss an individualized plan with a
doctor.
