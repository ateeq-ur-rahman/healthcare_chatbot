TITLE: Healthy Sleep and Stress Management
SOURCE: NIH / WHO (adapted educational summary)
---
Most adults need about 7-9 hours of sleep per night for good physical and
mental health. Consistently poor sleep is linked to a higher risk of weight
gain, weakened immunity, mood problems, and difficulty concentrating.

Tips for better sleep hygiene:
- Keep a consistent sleep and wake time, even on weekends.
- Avoid caffeine and heavy meals in the few hours before bedtime.
- Limit screen time (phones, laptops, TV) for 30-60 minutes before bed, as
  blue light can delay melatonin release.
- Keep the bedroom cool, dark, and quiet.
- Get natural light exposure during the day, which helps regulate the
  body's internal clock.
- Regular physical activity earlier in the day can improve sleep quality.

Managing everyday stress:
- Deep breathing exercises (e.g., slow inhale for 4 counts, hold for 4,
  exhale for 6) can activate the body's relaxation response.
- Regular physical activity, even a short daily walk, reduces stress
  hormones and improves mood.
- Maintaining social connections and talking about what's on your mind
  with someone you trust can reduce feelings of isolation.
- Structured routines and to-do lists can reduce the mental load of
  juggling many tasks.
- Mindfulness or meditation practices, even 5-10 minutes a day, have been
  associated with reduced stress and anxiety in general population
  studies.
- Limiting news/social media consumption during high-stress periods can
  help.

If stress, anxiety, or sleep problems are persistent, significantly
affecting daily life, or accompanied by low mood, please consult a doctor
or mental health professional — they can properly assess the situation and
discuss options such as therapy, which general lifestyle tips can't
replace.
