TITLE: Basic First Aid for Common Minor Injuries
SOURCE: Red Cross / WHO (adapted educational summary)
---
The information below covers general first aid for minor, non-emergency
situations. For any serious injury, heavy bleeding, difficulty breathing,
loss of consciousness, or suspected fracture/head injury, call emergency
services immediately rather than relying on self-care.

Minor cuts and scrapes:
1. Wash your hands before treating the wound if possible.
2. Rinse the wound gently with clean running water to remove dirt.
3. Apply gentle pressure with a clean cloth or sterile gauze to stop minor
   bleeding.
4. Apply an antiseptic and cover with a clean bandage.
5. Change the dressing daily and watch for signs of infection (increasing
   redness, warmth, swelling, pus, or fever).

Minor burns (first-degree, small area):
1. Cool the burn under cool (not ice-cold) running water for about 10-20
   minutes.
2. Do not apply ice, butter, or toothpaste to a burn.
3. Cover loosely with a clean, non-stick bandage.
4. Do not pop any blisters that form.
5. Seek medical care for burns larger than a small area, burns on the
   face/hands/joints, or any burn that blisters extensively or looks deep.

Minor sprains (RICE method):
- Rest the affected area and avoid putting weight on it.
- Ice the area for 15-20 minutes at a time, several times a day, wrapped
  in a cloth (never apply ice directly to skin).
- Compression with an elastic bandage can help reduce swelling.
- Elevate the injured limb above heart level when possible.
- Seek medical care if there is severe pain, inability to bear weight, or
  visible deformity.

Nosebleeds:
1. Sit up and lean slightly forward (not backward) to avoid swallowing
   blood.
2. Pinch the soft part of the nose firmly for 10-15 minutes.
3. Avoid blowing the nose for a few hours afterward.
4. Seek care if bleeding doesn't stop after 20-30 minutes or is very heavy.

This guidance is educational and general. It does not replace formal first
aid training or professional medical evaluation, especially for anything
beyond a clearly minor injury.
